# Topic Records

Teaching and assessment read and update the same topic record. This reference
describes its structure and what to record in it. To find, create, or verify a
record, follow the "Find and Update Records" section in
[`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md).

## Structure the Topic Record

For new notes, use the
[Obsidian template](../assets/obsidian/topic-note-obsidian.md) or the
[plain Markdown template](../assets/topic-note-markdown.md).

Repeat objective entries, outcome rows, examples, and gap entries as needed. In
the "Current Understanding" section, give each outcome its own row so that
assessment judgments refer to specific knowledge or skills. Link evidence to the
supporting session entries when available.

For source references, link exact page titles, preserving their capitalization,
and identify relevant portions outside the links.

For Obsidian records, use the `learning/topic` tag and relevant subject tags.
When existing subject vocabulary fits, reuse it. Keep subjects separate from the
`learning` hierarchy. Don't add a redundant `learning` parent tag, skill names,
or progress states. Follow `coding-standards` for tag syntax.

Plain Markdown has no tag metadata by default. When the destination has an
established convention, follow it.

Use `YYYY-MM-DD` dates for evidence, gap discovery and resolution, and session
entries. Keep session entries in chronological order.

Use the following sections for new topic notes:

| Section               | Contents                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Objectives            | What the user wants to understand or be able to do.                                                                          |
| Key Ideas             | Concise explanations, definitions, assumptions, and source references.                                                       |
| Examples              | Worked examples, applications, diagrams, or derivations covered during learning.                                             |
| Current Understanding | What the user has demonstrated independently, what required assistance, and what remains unassessed or ungraded.             |
| Open Gaps             | Demonstrated gaps and misconceptions, with discovery dates and links to the supporting session evidence.                     |
| Resume Point          | Where the session stopped and the next question, exercise, or concept to revisit.                                            |
| Log                   | Dated entries recording what was taught or assessed, the user's responses, assistance supplied, and the resulting judgments. |

Keep the current-state sections concise and update them as learning progresses.
Keep the log as a compact evidence history. Give each entry a heading that names
the specific topic or activity, not the date or session type. Beneath the
heading, use a two-column table with Date and Activity headers and one row of
metadata. Use `YYYY-MM-DD` dates and keep entries in chronological order.

When revisiting an activity, distinguish the heading by its purpose, such as
"Convergence Conditions: Delayed Review." If headings would otherwise be
identical, add a trailing date.

Update the same entry as a session or activity progresses. Saving after an
exercise doesn't require a new entry. Append an entry for a later session or a
distinct activity.

Use concise bullets to record the task, the substance of the user's response,
assistance supplied, and the supported conclusion. Preserve the distinction
between independent and assisted performance. Keep explanations in the "Key
Ideas" or "Examples" section and the next action in the "Resume Point" section
rather than repeating them in each log entry.

For existing notes, map this information to the user's structure rather than
imposing these headings or reorganizing unrelated content.

## Update Topic Records

When saving is enabled, update the topic record after a completed exercise, a
change in demonstrated understanding, or a pause or end to the session.

Record evidence rather than inferred mastery. Treat copied work and answers
completed with hints, corrections, or a supplied solution as assisted. Record
independent evidence from a fresh attempt without that support. Keep skipped
material unassessed and unresolved judgments ungraded.

During assessment, record answers and assistance without putting deferred
judgments or unrevealed solutions into learner-visible notes. Add that feedback
at the selected feedback time.

Mark an open gap resolved only after a fresh, unassisted question or application
demonstrates understanding. Preserve the earlier evidence and record the
resolution and its date.

If an agent error affected the record, correct the current understanding and add
a dated correction explaining which earlier judgment changed.

Update the "Resume Point" section and the relevant log entry. Append a new entry
for a later session or a distinct activity.
